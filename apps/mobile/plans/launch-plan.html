02 Connection.

I read the connection code before planning. It's already smarter than I expected: every route is probed in parallel, and a network change or seeing the Mac on Wi-Fi wakes it up straight away. So this track is small, and it targets the two costs that matter.

ChangeWhyWhere
Built-in Tailscale starts only when Wi-Fi can't reach the Mac, and stops after 3 minutes back on Wi-FiToday, once it's on, the embedded node runs from launch even at home. That costs battery and data for nothing.packages/app/src/connection/session.ts, tunnel.ts
Fallback "is Wi-Fi back?" check every 5 minutes instead of every minuteNetwork changes and seeing the Mac on Wi-Fi already trigger the switch immediately. The timer is only a safety net.packages/client-runtime/src/connection.ts
Not doing, and why:
  • A lighter heartbeat RPC. It would need a protocol change, the hello it replaces is already small, and older Macs would need a fallback.
  • "Try the last route first". Routes are already probed in parallel, so it would save nothing.
  • Push alerts through Firebase. That's a privacy change for you to decide later.

03 Privacy policy.

The page at /privacy covers the Mac app well, but it's behind on the phone app and on Remote. These are the changes, each checked against the code:

SectionChange
Short version, §1Readings history is a SQLite file now, ~/.vitals/history/history.sqlite3, not a CSV
§7 RemoteRemote also works directly over your Wi-Fi, encrypted end to end and pinned to your Mac's key. It is not only Tailscale any more.
New: the phone appWhat the phone stores, and each permission and why it's needed:
  • What it stores: the pairing, a session token and the device key, all in secure storage.
  • Camera: only to read the pairing QR, processed on the phone.
  • Local network: to find your Mac on Wi-Fi.
  • Notifications: local only, with no push service.
It also sends your phone's name to your own Mac.
New: update checksThe app checks Expo's update server (u.expo.dev) for new versions. That request carries a random per-install ID, the platform and the app version. Google Play can also offer store updates.
New: QR scannerOn Android the scanner uses Google ML Kit. ML Kit sends Google diagnostics: device model, OS, app version, a per-install ID and performance metrics. No images leave the phone.
New: built-in TailscaleOnly if you turn it on. The phone joins your tailnet through Tailscale's servers, and Tailscale's standard client logs are uploaded to Tailscale. Their privacy policy is linked.
Updated date"Last updated" moves to the day this ships

How they're made

  • One system. The new phone screens and the Mac window become coded components on the website. The same components render the homepage section, every store image and the promo video. The homepage phones get updated to the new design along the way, because today they still show the old Chart, Orders and Ward tabs.
  • Exact pixels. Each asset is a render-only route, for example /brand/render/feature-graphic, at the exact store size. A script uses playwright-core (already a dev dependency) to save the PNGs and record the video. Then ffmpeg makes the MP4s, and the script builds the zips.
  • Honest numbers. Your rules say readings are never invented, so the screens use one real snapshot from your Mac (CPU, memory, fans, processes), captured once and stored as fixture data. I'd capture it with your OK.

10 Your decisions.

  1. Look: A Pulse line, B Big number, or C Night desk. Mixing is fine, for example "C with A's line".
  2. Tagline: "Your Mac, in your pocket." or one of your own.
  3. Real snapshot: OK to pair the emulator with your Mac once so the assets show true numbers? That means one Allow click on your Mac.
  4. Upload key: keep Lumo's, or generate a dedicated Vitals key now? The dedicated key is my recommendation.
  5. Tailscale logs: keep the upload (and disclose it), or turn it off?
  6. Play account: personal or organization? That decides whether the 14-day closed test applies.
