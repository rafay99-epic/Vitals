Vitals Share: one clipboard, any file, both ways

Two features that work together across your Mac, your Android phone and your iPhone. Shared clipboard: copy on one device, paste on the other, plus a history. Files: LocalSend-style transfers of any size and type (APKs, videos, photos, documents, whole folders), Mac to phone and phone to Mac, over your Wi-Fi or Tailscale. The clipboard part shipped in PR #165; this plan finishes it and adds files. Written 2026-10-24.

1. Shared clipboard, both ways

Mac to phone (shipped in #165, polished here)

  • The Mac records what you copy (off by default, passwords never).
  • The phone shows the latest copy on Control and the full history under Clipboard.
  • "Copy Mac copies here": while Vitals is open, each new Mac copy lands on the phone clipboard, repeat copies included.
  • New: ⌃⌥⌘V on the Mac sends the current clipboard to the phone right away, even with history off.

Phone to Mac (new)

  • When you open Vitals: Android reads the new copy and shows "You copied a link · Send to Mac", or sends it straight away if "Send phone copies to Mac" is on. iPhone knows something new was copied without reading it, and one tap on Apple's Paste button sends it, with no "Allow Paste" prompt.
  • Without opening Vitals: a Quick Settings tile on Android, "Send clipboard" (a brief see-through screen does the read, since Android only lets the app in front read the clipboard). On iPhone, a Shortcuts action "Send clipboard to Mac" that you can put on Back Tap.
  • On the Mac, the copy lands on the clipboard (⌘V) and in the menu bar panel: "Pixel 9 Pro copied: … · Copy".

What no app can do: neither Android nor iOS lets an app read or write the clipboard in the background. So there is no silent, always-on two-way sync, and the plan doesn't pretend otherwise. The tile, the Shortcut and the open-the-app path are the honest versions.

2. Files, both ways

Details of the transport, options considered, and security are in the Drop transport plan. In short: small messages stay on today's connection, while file bytes go over HTTPS, read and written by native code. On Wi-Fi the phone checks the Mac's certificate fingerprint, which it learned over the paired connection. Over Tailscale it uses Tailscale's normal ts.net certificate.

3. Phone interaction (Android and iPhone, one code path)

  • Control › Share: the Mac's latest copy (Copy is one tap), a live line while files arrive ("Getting 3 files from your Mac · 34%"), then four tiles: Clipboard, Files, Open link, Find Mac.
  • Clipboard screen: as shipped, plus "Send phone copies to Mac" and the copy strip.
  • Files screen: where things land; Send tiles (Files, Photos, Folder); live transfer cards with Cancel; "From your Mac" and "Sent" lists; "Start without asking".
  • File sheet: Install (APK, Android), Save to Photos (photos and videos), Open or Show in Files, Share, Remove from list. Apps get a clear warning.
  • Share sheet: text and links go to the Clipboard Send sheet. Files open the Files Send sheet, where "Mac clipboard" is turned off with a reason when the file is too big.
  • Leaving the app: Android keeps going with a progress notification, and the Quick Settings tile is always there. iPhone gets about 30 seconds, then pauses and says "Open Vitals to finish".

4. Mac interaction

  • Four ways in, one list: drop on the menu bar icon, drop on the Dock icon, Finder › Quick Actions › Send to Phone (with Services › Send to Phone with Vitals as the fallback), or Remote › Sharing › Send Files…. All four end in the same transfer list.
  • Phone picker: it only appears when more than one phone has Files allowed. The last-used phone is pre-checked, and you can tick both. Offline phones are listed as "Offline, it will wait".
  • What opens after Send: a menu bar drop opens the panel; a Dock drop opens Remote › Sharing; a Quick Action opens nothing. The menu bar shows the percent next to its icon, and the Dock tile shows a progress bar.
  • Menu bar panel: a Share section at the top appears only while something is happening: asks (Accept, Decline), running transfers ("0.5 of 1.4 GB · Wi-Fi", pause, cancel), "Waiting for Pixel 9 Pro…", the last finished drop (Show in Finder), and "Pixel 9 Pro copied: … · Copy". The bottom row gains a Send clipboard button labelled with its shortcut.
  • Notifications: Got (Show in Finder), Wants to send (Accept, Decline), Sent, Waiting (Cancel) and Clipboard sent. No banners while the panel is open, because it already shows the same thing.
  • Remote: segments become Devices | Sharing | Activity | Pressure Guard. Each phone in Devices gets a Files checkbox. Sharing holds a Files card (save folder, start without asking, ask for browsers, largest drop, Send Files…), a Recent list (Running, Paused, Waiting, Asking, Done, Failed with Retry, Cancelled, Declined) and the Clipboard card.
  • Shortcut: ⌃⌥⌘V sends the Mac clipboard to the phone. It can be re-recorded in Settings › Shortcuts, and "Sends to" can be the last-used phone, a specific phone or all phones.
  • Gatekeeper: received DMG, PKG and app files show "App · quarantined" ("macOS will check it before it runs"). Every received file gets the quarantine flag, as PR #165 already does.

5. Web client

  • The Orders clipboard sheet (shipped) keeps history, drop and paste.
  • Files: downloads become direct HTTPS links with the session cookie, so the browser handles them natively. Uploads are streamed PUTs. Browsers always ask the Mac to accept.

7. Honest limits

  • No background clipboard sync on phones: OS rules, explained in the app.
  • The Mac can't wake the phone app: no push service by design. Drops wait, and Android's 15-minute check can start them.
  • iPhone pauses about 30 seconds after you leave the app on Wi-Fi.
  • Speeds are measured in S8 and not promised before then.
  • The Stable Install button needs Google's approval.
