Vitals Recent

Goal: get a screenshot, photo, document or clipboard text from any of your devices to any other in two taps, without WhatsApp. It goes over your own Wi-Fi or Tailscale, through your Mac, with no cloud copy. Plan written 2026-10-27. Nothing here is built yet. Three UX directions are in section 3; pick one.

The pieces exist on feat/connect-phone-files (#170) and the Drop and Clipboard PRs. The problem is the path to them: they are spread over four screens and you have to know which folder a file is in.

2. The idea: one "Recent" list per device

You almost never want "a file in a folder". You want the screenshot I just took, the PDF I downloaded this morning or the text I copied a minute ago. So instead of a folder tree, every device exposes a short, newest-first list in five kinds:

KindAndroid sourceiPhone sourceMac source
ScreenshotsMediaStore, Pictures/Screenshots + DCIM/ScreenshotsPhotos, Screenshots smart albumDesktop + screencapture location
Photos and videosMediaStore images and videoPhotos library (needs read access)Pictures folder
DownloadsDownload/ by dateFiles › Vitals~/Downloads by date
DocumentsMediaStore by type: PDF, Office, textFiles › Vitals~/Documents by date
ClipboardCopies sent from this phoneCopies sent from this phoneClipboard history (already built)

The full folder browser stays, one tap down, for the rare "it's somewhere in there" case.

Why this beats WhatsApp for this

  • Never leaves your devices. Bytes go phone → your Mac → phone over your LAN (pinned TLS) or Tailscale (WireGuard). No server holds a copy.
  • Full quality. WhatsApp re-compresses photos and strips metadata unless you send them as documents. Vitals sends the original file.
  • You decide who sees what. The per-device access presets on the Mac (Watch, Control, Everything) already gate files; Recent uses the same files scope.
  • No chat to scroll. You don't send yourself a message and dig it out later; you just pick it from the other device.

Short version: Android and Mac can do all of it, all the time. iPhone can do all of it while Vitals is open, and falls back to Share › Vitals when it isn't. That's an iOS rule, not something we can code around without a paid Apple team (and even then only for Files, not background Photos).

5. How it's built

P1 · Recent RPC~2 days
One new contract, fs.recent { kinds, limit, before } → { id, kind, name, size, at, thumb }[], under the existing files scope. The gateway relays it to phones exactly like fs.list does in #170, and fs.read gets the bytes. Android answers from MediaStore through expo-media-library (already installed) plus a date-sorted Download listing. iPhone answers from PhotoKit while open; we flip photosPermission on in app.config.ts. Mac answers from its shared folders + screenshot location. Gateway test: a phone asks for the other phone's screenshots and gets them newest first.
P2 · The picked screen~2 days
Phone: the direction you pick (A or B) replaces Control › Files as the first thing in Files. Folder browser moves under "All files". Mac: the same in Remote, plus drag-out to Finder (file promises, so the download starts on drop) and ⌘C to copy an image. Space bar Quick Look.
P3 · Clipboard everywhere~1–2 days
Clipboard rows show which device they came from. Android: a "Send clipboard" Quick Settings tile and notification action (a see-through activity reads the clipboard and closes, the same trick KDE Connect uses). "Copy Mac copies here" keeps working with Vitals closed through the service. iPhone: a Share › Vitals text action. Passwords stay skipped everywhere, as today.
P4 · Arrives on its own~2 days, optional
The toggles from direction C, all off by default. Android: the service watches MediaStore for new screenshots and sends them to the Mac (Downloads › Vitals › Screenshots) and onto the Mac clipboard. Mac: watches its screenshot folder and offers the newest to phones. iPhone: sends new screenshots when you open Vitals.

Each phase is its own branch from nightly, verified on the Android emulator against the fake Mac and on Vitals Dev, with an APK for you after each.
